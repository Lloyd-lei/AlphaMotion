Executive conclusion

The current single-seed evidence does not yet support a claim that Greenwich is easier to train than traditional retargeting on this G1 set. Retargeting passes 16/18 versus Greenwich 14/18, has stronger 0–1500 learning AUC and success at iteration 1500, and has lower mean body, joint, and support-slip errors; the median first iteration reaching 90% success is tied at 500. Greenwich nevertheless demonstrates high absolute trainability across diverse motions, including jumping and dance: every motion reaches at least 96.9% best completion success and 14/18 pass the fixed multi-metric gate. The correct external claim today is therefore high cross-motion trainability, not superiority over retargeting. The two running additional seeds are required before a final comparative claim.

What is proven, and what is not

  • Proven by the completed seed: both pipelines produce broadly trainable G1 references under one frozen controller/reward configuration. Greenwich reaches ≥96.9% best episode success on all 18 motions; retargeting reaches 100% on all 18.
  • Not yet proven: statistical superiority, GPU-cost superiority, or robust generalization across seeds. Two additional seeds are still running.
  • Not measurable from the present artifacts: source-human semantic error, contact precision/recall/F1, exact foot contact area, self-collision count, penetration depth, torque saturation fraction, and exact per-job GPU cost. These require source annotations or additional simulator logging and are not inferred here.
  • Torque interpretation: reported values are MuJoCo position-actuator forces in actuation space. The current logs store p95 and absolute maximum, not the fraction of frames above rated torque.

Protocol

Each paired motion uses the same G1 model, 4,096 environments, 50 Hz reference, PPO architecture, reward terms, domain randomization, evaluation schedule, and fixed quality gate. Evaluation uses 64 episodes across a full-clip phase and four fixed suffix phases; a trial ends at first failure with no reset splicing. Minimum training is 1,500 iterations, with continuation to 3,000 or 5,000 when the convergence gate is not met.

Fixed quality gate: success ≥95%, body error ≤120 mm, joint RMSE ≤0.35 rad, anchor orientation error ≤0.4 rad, support-slip proxy ≤10%, plus phase-sliced completion requirements.
