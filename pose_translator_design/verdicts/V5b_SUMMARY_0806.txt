  D  is the mechanism test, and it half-confirmed: share 0.11 -> 0.57 (window-specific benefit

     clean-confirmation criterion is NOT met. Neither is the alternative ("aug destroys
     regardless"): share clearly un-collapsed once the shortcut was removed.

HONEST READ
  1. No arm gets (i) and (ii) high at once. The interventions move along a tradeoff frontier
     between "token is speed-invariant / decoder leans on endpoints" and "token carries this
     window's motion"; nothing broke the frontier. Absolute window-specific benefit stays

  2. 2b is pinned at 0.63-0.71 across every aug variant we have ever run. The 2b exam's two

  4. The prefix ladder stayed perfectly flat in all pathint-family arms — the OAT ordering

  NEXT (if continued): B + a gentler D (e.g. 0.15/0.05) WITHOUT C, and consider decoupling

PLAIN VERDICT
  1. The 0.95 acceptance line was impossible. A perfect speed-removing descriptor that keeps
     the window's content tops out at 0.37-0.42 on this exam once you subtract the shared-
     mean-pose inflation that the tokens provably do not have (their controls are ~0). Even
     WITH that inflation the ceiling is 0.958 — and only because unrelated windows already
     score 0.78 under it. The two windows genuinely differ by 30 frames; no content-carrying
     token can reach 0.95 with a near-zero control.
  2. The aug arms are not "at ~95-100% of achievable" — they are 1.5-2x PAST it. 0.70 on a
     control-matched scale where the ideal geometric descriptor gets 0.36-0.42 means the extra
     similarity does not come from better speed invariance; it comes from DISCARDING window
     content (or over-weighting the shared prefix). This independently confirms the content-
     share collapse (0.11-0.28) from the geometry side: 2b > ~0.4 and content share > 0.7 are
     mathematically in tension ON THIS EXAM.
  3. So the exam, not the training, is what needs revising: past ~0.4, recipe-2b cosine is an
     inverse content meter, not an invariance meter. A replacement exam should compare tokens
     of the SAME raw span at different readout speeds with content held fixed (2a does this
     but is trivially passed by construction) — e.g. time-warped readouts of one span (the
     warp_idx machinery already exists) with acceptance re-set against this oracle, which
     scores 1.00 there. Chasing 0.95 on the current 2b with more training-side iterations
     would only reward further content discarding.

RECIPE (LIT_JEPA_COLLAPSE.md finding 4, family 4):
  guided = decode(cond, null_token) + w * (decode(cond, token) - decode(cond, null_token))
  extrapolated on the DECODER OUTPUT (per-frame key-joint SE3): positions linear,
  rotations GEODESIC (rotvec scaling of R_tok R_null^-1, scipy), re-emitted 6D.
  w=1 reproduces the plain decode bit-for-bit (checked vs the verdict's prefix8 row).

PREMISE CAVEAT, stated up front: the lit note's "already learned decode(cond, null-token)"
is INVERTED relative to what train_v5.py actually does. cdrop drops the ENDPOINT COND
(cond_null embeddings) — the model learned decode(null_cond, token), the reverse branch.
The token side was only ever PARTIALLY dropped (OAT prefix mask, budgets 1/2/4/8 — never
0 visible registers). So the null-token branch used here is an untrained extrapolation of
two mechanically clean paths, both run:
  hid  = all 8 path registers key_padding_masked (training's own mask mechanism at budget 0)
  zero = path_z = 0 (the standard verdict's tokens_zeroed row; reproduces its 9.46 cm)

VERDICT: NO w>1 is strictly better. Guidance moves the model along a trade-off line,
it does not dominate w=1:
  - Content: REAL but small. The shuffle gap does grow monotonically (0.85 -> 1.40 cm),
    so the extrapolation direction genuinely amplifies window-specific token content —
    the mechanism works as the lit note predicted.
  - Cost: error grows faster than content (7.89 -> 18.86 cm), jitter accel +71% by w=5,
    and output warp-invariance decays 0.920 -> 0.843. The energy gap closes (0.79 -> 1.0
    around w~2.5) but by amplifying noise as much as motion.
  - Best non-trivial point: w=1.5/hid — energy +0.046, pathint marginally better
    (0.054 vs 0.055), shuffle gap +0.26 cm, for +0.43 cm error and +7% accel. A defensible
    knob if downstream wants livelier decode; not a free win.
  - Root cause visible in the null row itself: the token-free decode (hid) already scores
    8.93 cm / 0.784 energy — barely worse than the full decode. The token's marginal
    contribution in OUTPUT space is ~1 cm, so there is little signal for guidance to
    amplify before it amplifies the residual noise. Guidance cannot create content the
    decoder never routed through the tokens; it can only turn up the volume on what is
    there. The fix stays on the training side (gentler cdrop + 2c-judged content recipe
    above), with w~1.5 available as a cheap inference-time garnish afterwards.
