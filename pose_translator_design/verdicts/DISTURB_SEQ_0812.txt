DISTURBANCE AS A SEGMENT (0812) — lloyd's formulation:
    A_curr --bridge--> B_init [ REAL disturbance segment ] B_target --bridge--> A_target
The disturbance is a MOTION, not a pose. The model builds both seams itself.
9 reps, disturbance families jump / kick / dance, |B| = 45 frames, frozen stack.
Endpoint pass-through is ON at the seams (that is its correct use — we are
joining to a real segment) and OFF on the final leg, or arrival is 0.00 by
construction. (Both earlier versions of this test reported such a 0.00; both
are corrected here.)

metric              value
clean_arrive         3.51      (A_curr -> A_target, same total length)
seq_arrive           3.57      after being taken through a foreign motion
seqF_arrive          3.58      same, with budgets the model chose itself
clean_maxstep        4.08      largest single-frame step a CLEAN motion takes
seam_in              1.92      largest step around the entry seam
seam_out             1.65      largest step around the exit seam
seq_maxstep          7.82      largest step anywhere (includes the real B seg)
clean_jerk           0.35   seq_jerk 1.09   seqF_jerk 0.78

READING
 * THE SEAMS ARE THE SMOOTHEST PART. seam-in 1.92 and seam-out 1.65 are both
   BELOW the 4.08 that an undisturbed motion already produces in its worst
   frame. lloyd's continuity requirement (curr -> curr+1 must stay close) is
   met, and met with margin, once the disturbance is a segment the model
   bridges into and out of.
 * DETOUR COSTS ALMOST NOTHING IN ARRIVAL: 3.57 vs 3.51 clean (+1.7%). Being
   dragged through a whole foreign motion — a jump, a kick, a dance — and then
   sent back does not degrade where it ends up.
 * THE PRICE IS SMOOTHNESS IN THE MIDDLE, NOT AT THE JOINTS: overall jerk 1.09
   vs 0.35 clean. Letting the model pick its own budgets cuts that to 0.78
   (-29%) at equal arrival, the same direction the pose-pin version showed.
 * n CHOSEN BY THE MODEL SPREADS WIDELY (in/out pairs 100/80, 60/30, 80/20,
   30/100, 45/20, 100/100, ...). It is not collapsing to one value; entry and
   exit budgets differ per case, which is what "the target is the identity, n
   is only pacing" predicts.
PER-FAMILY NOTE: dance disturbances give the roughest seams (2.5-3.3 vs clean
0.7-1.6 in those reps) while jump/kick seams stay under 1.2. Worth a look if
this becomes a product path.
