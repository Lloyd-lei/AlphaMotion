WHY THIS IS BEING REDONE
  The first verdict turned on PRE-projection joint-limit violations. That criterion
  is wrong for this system: manifold_project(method="global") is a mandatory layer in
  the production path, it is not optional post-processing, and it is measured here to
  drive post-projection violations to 0.0000% for BOTH encoders on every one of the
  six target bodies. A violation the projection removes never reaches the robot. The
  quantity that reaches the robot is the POST-projection pose, and nobody had measured
  it. Below it is measured on 3000-6000 frames per body, on the seed-0 LAFAN val split
  AND on cache_amass_heldout (600 clips, disjoint from every training corpus).

   THE pal_talos NOTE — this is not a body, it is a naming failure.
   pal_talos's cached joint names are "body00","body01",...,"body32". Every joint name
   carries ZERO semantic content. It is the one embodiment in the cache where the text
   encoder has literally nothing to encode, so the joint embeddings are arbitrary
   functions of "the body07 joint of a body" and whichever encoder happens to land in a
   luckier arrangement wins by chance. Both arms score 40-48 deg on it (vs 15-26 on G1)
   and 44-57 deg on its own SELF-reconstruction. It is the only body in 18 where Qwen
   loses, and it is the body where the encoder comparison is meaningless by
   construction. Fix the names in the cache before using pal_talos to judge anything.

   ALSO: unitree_g1_23dof is a DESCRIPTOR DUPLICATE of unitree_g1_29dof — identical
   joint_names, identical rest_offsets, bit-identical _dof.npz (both list 29 DOF). Only
   the GT motion differs. So the two G1 rows are one body evaluated against two
   retargets, not two independent bodies. Their projection-movement numbers are
   identical for exactly this reason. Do not count them as two votes.

VERDICT
  QWEN WINS on post-projection quality. The earlier "keep SigLIP" call was made on
  pre-projection violations, and that criterion does not survive contact with the
  projection layer: it is partly a chart artifact, it vanishes entirely in the
  chart-free infeasibility measurement, and it is zero for both arms after the
  mandatory projection every production pose passes through. It should be retired as a
  model-selection criterion. lloyd's eye and the two objective numbers he cited were
  right; the number that contradicted them was not measuring a defect.

  DOES THE MARGIN CLEAR THE VARIANCE BAND? Two answers, and they differ — state both.
  * BY MAGNITUDE, MOSTLY NO. Post-projection zero-shot error on G1 moves 0.22-1.20 deg.
    The measured run-to-run band is 2.2 deg. Taken one number at a time, the decisive
    metric is UNRESOLVED. The single exception is human self-reconstruction on the
    clean external exam: -4.66 deg (22.97 -> 18.31), which clears 2.2 deg outright.
  * BY DIRECTION, YES. The comparison is not one number, it is ~40 of them. Qwen is
    ahead on 17 of 18 embodiments, on two disjoint corpora, and the one exception is a
    body whose joints are literally named body00..body32. If the effect were run noise
    the signs would scatter; they do not (sign test p = 1.4e-4 per corpus). Qwen is
    also ahead on both G1 post-projection cells on both corpora, on projection movement
    on held-out AMASS (7.78 vs 10.34), on arm projection movement on all three
    vendor-MJCF bodies, and on true infeasibility on the two hardest ones. A small
    effect measured consistently is still a real effect.
    Plainly: Qwen is better by roughly 0.3-1.0 deg on cross-body decode and ~4.7 deg on
    source-side representation quality. It is not a transformation. It is a real,
    reproducible, one-directional improvement.

  RECOMMENDATION FOR THE PRODUCTION RETRAIN: use Qwen3.
  * It is ahead on every metric that reaches the robot, and behind on none once
    pal_talos is set aside for cause.
  * The cost argument in the old verdict does not survive either: the two checkpoints
    are 18.006M vs 18.071M parameters (+0.36%), the text tower is FROZEN and its output
    is a 300-entry disk LUT, so inference cost is identical and the extra Qwen forward
    pass happens once per vocabulary change, not per frame.
  * Two caveats to carry forward, neither blocking:
    - SigLIP is consistently better on the reach/manipulation family (+1.7 deg, n=800,
      both G1s). If the production target is manipulation-led rather than
      locomotion-led, re-measure on a manipulation-heavy corpus before committing.
    - The margin is inside the run-to-run band per-metric. If the retrain is expensive,
      the cheap way to remove all doubt is 3 seeds x 2 encoders on the same recipe;
      that turns "17/18 bodies point the same way" into a proper confidence interval.

  THREE DATA BUGS FOUND WHILE MEASURING — these matter more than the encoder choice:
  1. pal_talos joint names in cache_xwbc_lafan and cache_amass_heldout are
     body00..body32. The name-semantics channel — the whole mechanism this experiment
     was testing — is dead for that body. Its 40-57 deg errors are not a model result.
     Re-extract its names from the MJCF and rebuild both caches.
  2. unitree_g1_23dof carries unitree_g1_29dof's descriptor verbatim (same joint_names,
     same rest_offsets, same _dof.npz listing 29 DOF). Every "two held-out G1s" result
     in this and the previous verdict is one body scored twice.
  3. agibot_a3_ultra_t3d0 (J=60) and apptronik_apollo take 39-50 deg of projection
     movement in the wrists/torso for BOTH encoders. That is a descriptor/topology
     problem in those bodies, not an encoder problem, and it is the largest single
     source of zero-shot error in this whole report. It deserves its own investigation.

  WHAT STILL DOES NOT MOVE: the axis question. Nothing here contradicts the first
  verdict's section B — a bigger text tower does not separate hip pitch/roll/yaw. That
  conclusion stands, and geometry/DOF conditioning is still the right lever for it.
