DOES RETARGETING DISTORT ARMS MORE THAN LEGS? (0813)
Measured on the paired labels only — no model anywhere. Angle between the
human's root->limb-end direction and the robot's, using the codebase's own
KEY_PATTERNS semantic joints (root/wrist/ankle), which are the correspondences
the whole cross-embodiment design already relies on. 24 windows x 60 frames.

robot            l_wrist  r_wrist  l_ankle  r_ankle | wrist/ankle
unitree_h1          60.0     67.8     24.6     26.7 |    2.49
booster_t1          27.8     53.3     27.5     28.8 |    1.44
fourier_gr3         26.0     35.1     26.4     28.7 |    1.11
hightorque_hi       31.6     59.3     25.0     28.8 |    1.69
ALL                 43.4 (wrist)      26.4 (ankle)  |    1.64

ANSWER: YES, and by a clear margin. Retargeting moves the hand direction 43
degrees away from the human's on average, and the foot direction 26. Arms are
distorted 1.64x more than legs, on every robot tested (1.11 to 2.49).
Ankles are remarkably consistent across bodies (24.6-28.8, a 17% spread) while
wrists vary hugely (26.0-67.8, a 2.6x spread) — the legs are being solved the
same way everywhere, the arms are not.
ASYMMETRY WORTH NOTING: the RIGHT wrist is worse than the left on all four
bodies (67.8 vs 60.0, 53.3 vs 27.8, 35.1 vs 26.0, 59.3 vs 31.6). A left/right
asymmetry in a geometry-driven transform is not something geometry explains;
it points at the solver or at a handedness bias in the source data.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE
 * It settles lloyd's summary: the labels do distort arms most. Combined with
   the readout probe (codes -> human wrist 12 cm, codes -> paired robot wrist
   30 cm), the arm ceiling is a label property, and the four latent-side
   eliminations (decoder capacity, decoder training, loss wiring, code budget)
   are consistent with that.
 * It does NOT establish that the distortion is ERROR. A robot's shoulder is
   not a human's, so some direction change is required, not wrong. What the
   numbers do show is that the change is (a) much larger for arms than legs,
   (b) far more variable across bodies for arms, and (c) left/right asymmetric —
   and (b) and (c) are hard to explain by geometry alone.

RETRACTION ATTACHED: the earlier SEW decomposition (SEW_DECOMPOSE_0813) reported
label-vs-human arm direction errors of 36-49 deg using shoulder/elbow/wrist
triples picked by a keyword-plus-depth heuristic. Printing the picks showed they
were wrong (human arm resolved as [Elbow, Wrist, Shoulder]; h1's leg as two hip
joints with the knee missing). Those numbers are withdrawn. The roll-vs-direction
split in that file is withdrawn with them. This file replaces it, using only
correspondences the codebase itself defines.
