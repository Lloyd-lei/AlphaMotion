FK-LOSS / ROT-ONLY ARMS — VERDICT (0813). Everything measured on the FK
yardstick (FK on predicted rotations vs FK on GT rotations), the only ruler
that is meaningful for rot_only models: their hpos head is gone, so the
training eval's own pos_cm column measures |GT| and is discarded.
All four arms share the PROD_dim16_13k_0809 recipe item for item.

model                    slots  bits/f | arm mean  arm p95  leg mean  wrist p95
PROD_dim16_13k (base)      256   12288 |    6.78    29.28     4.21      37.63
SMALL_dim16_reenc          256   12288 |    7.17    29.44     4.37      37.62
FK1  rot-only 128          128    6144 |    6.62    28.51     4.37      37.60
FK2  rot-only 256          256   12288 |    6.81    28.54     4.21      37.18

RESULT 1 — HALF THE BUDGET IS GENUINELY FREE. FK1 uses 128 slots and 6144
bits/frame, HALF the baseline's storage, and is not worse: arm mean -2.3%,
arm p95 -2.6%, wrist p95 -0.1%, legs +3.8%. The frame-level ablation showed the
position codes cannot reach the render; this shows that removing them costs
nothing end to end. Greenwich can be halved.

RESULT 2 — DOUBLING THE USEFUL CAPACITY BUYS ALMOST NOTHING. FK2 spends the
same 12288 bits as the baseline but puts all of them on rotation. Against the
baseline: arm p95 -2.5%, wrist p95 -1.2%, legs flat. Against FK1 (half the
bits) it is within noise on arms and slightly better on legs (14.00 vs 14.59)
and wrists (37.18 vs 37.60). So the extra 128 rotation slots are worth ~1-4%,
not the step change the "half the budget is wasted" framing might suggest.
Reading: at this scale the bottleneck is NOT code capacity.

RESULT 3 — THE FK LOSS DID NOT RESCUE THE ARMS EITHER. The hypothesis was that
the metric, lever-arm-aware signal was being delivered to a discarded head, and
that rewiring it into the rotation head would fix the arm tail. Both FK arms
move arm p95 by about -2.5%. Real, reproducible, and far too small to be the
arm story. Combined with the earlier decoder post-trains (<1%) and the readout
probe (codes -> paired robot wrist saturates around 30 cm), the arm tail is
now unexplained by: decoder capacity, decoder training, loss wiring, and code
budget. What remains on the table is the paired-label variance itself, which
is where the GMR audit pointed.

WHAT TO ADOPT ANYWAY
 * rot_only: halves Greenwich's storage, halves Equator's input width, halves
   what Stage B must predict — at no measured cost. This is a free efficiency
   win independent of the arm question, and it should go into the next build.
 * fk_loss: keep it. It costs nothing, it supervises the head that actually
   renders, and it removes a genuinely confusing piece of architecture (a
   5.0-weighted loss on a discarded output).
 * The reenc arm remains the one with a large effect (round-trip 11% -> 61%);
   note it is slightly WORSE here on arms (+5.8% mean), consistent with the
   earlier finding that it spends generalisation headroom.
STILL TO RUN: arm 3 (foot contact in the loss), which is the only one of
lloyd's three that has not been tested, and which needs the contact labels
built carefully first.

=== ADDENDUM 0813: AMPLITUDE RATIO, AND A RETRACTION ===
lloyd watched the punch render and said the robot barely moved except one arm.
A single-clip measurement seemed to confirm a severe amplitude collapse (all
models at 30-44% of GT range, legs at 15-25%), and I reported it as a general
finding. Measured properly it is NOT general.

Full measurement, 24 contiguous 90-frame val windows x 4 cross-body directions,
FK positions, ratio = std_over_time(decoded) / std_over_time(GT):
model                        arm err  arm p95 | amp all  amp arm  amp leg
PROD_c1_91k (Greenwich)         6.54    24.74 |  0.888    0.897    0.954
PROD_dim16_13k (baseline)       7.08    31.19 |  0.912    0.935    0.941
SMALL_dim16_reenc               7.50    31.45 |  0.898    0.919    0.911
FK1 rot-only 128                6.90    29.95 |  0.895    0.910    0.949
FK2 rot-only 256                7.06    30.14 |  0.885    0.905    0.937
PT_c1dec_reenc                  6.56    25.01 |  0.894    0.903    0.950
Amplitude is preserved at 88-95% everywhere, and the arms are not the weak
group on this ruler — the legs are marginally the BEST preserved.

Is collapse amplitude-dependent? 300 windows of C1, human->G1, binned by GT
amplitude:
  GT std 2.1 cm -> retention 0.985      GT std 11.1 cm -> 0.868
  GT std 5.4 cm -> 0.901                GT std 16.4 cm -> 0.941
  corr(GT amplitude, retention) = -0.073   top decile 0.937, bottom decile 1.122
No trend. The hypothesis that big motions get compressed more is REFUTED.

RETRACTION: the "all models compress motion to 30-44%" claim came from ONE
punch window and does not survive contact with 24 (or 300). What is real: on
that particular clip the decode was poor, and a single window has enormous
variance. What is NOT real: a systematic amplitude collapse. I reported the
single-clip number as a general finding before measuring it at scale; that was
the same mistake this project has a written rule against.

WHAT STANDS
 * The amplitude ratio is worth keeping in the battery (dual_common.
   amplitude_report). It is cheap, it is temporal where evaluate() is not, and
   it would have caught a real collapse had there been one. It now also serves
   as the control that says there isn't.
 * The visual observation still needs an explanation: something about that punch
   render read as static. Candidates not yet separated: that clip's decode
   really is bad (its arm error was 19-21 cm against a 6-7 cm corpus mean), the
   in-place rendering with no world translation makes locomotion invisible, or
   the camera framing. Worth one more look before concluding.
