SEW DECOMPOSITION (0813) — where the arm error actually lives.
Motivated by SEW-Mimic (arXiv 2602.01632): matching an end effector's POSITION
leaves the arm under-determined, so part of a paired label's arm configuration
is solver choice rather than human motion. Split the error into DIRECTION
(segment vectors, determined by the human) and ROLL (rotation about the limb
axis, invisible to segment positions and free for the solver to pick).
C1, human -> unitree_g1_29dof, 8 windows x 60 frames per family, degrees.

       |  upper-arm  forearm    roll      | upper-arm  forearm

hard families: direction 34.9  roll 29.0  (roll = 45% of the total)
easy families: direction 17.9  roll 14.2  (roll = 44% of the total)

FINDING 1 — ROLL IS NOT THE STORY. The roll share is 45% on hard families and
44% on easy ones: identical. If arbitrary redundancy resolution were what makes
the hard families hard, roll would carry a larger share there. It does not. The
hard families are hard in DIRECTION, which is the component the human motion
actually determines. So the SEW mechanism, as a family-level explanation, is
not supported — the same outcome as the joint-limit test.

FINDING 2 — AND THIS IS THE LARGE ONE. The LABEL diverges from the HUMAN far
more than our MODEL diverges from the LABEL. Upper-arm direction, label vs
human: 27-83 degrees, mean 48.8 on hard families and 36.2 on easy ones —
against a model-vs-label direction error of 34.9 / 17.9. The retargeting step
changes the arm's direction by roughly TWICE what our model then adds.
That is measured on the labels themselves with no model in the loop.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE LATENT-SPACE FIX lloyd asked for
The model is being trained to reproduce an arm direction that already departs
from the human's by 36-49 degrees. Some of that departure is legitimate (the
robot's shoulder geometry differs and cannot copy a human arm exactly), and
some is solver behaviour. We cannot separate those two from this measurement
alone, and it would be wrong to assume either.
What we CAN say: the largest single term in the arm story is the human->robot
direction change in the labels, not anything in our latent space. Every
latent-side lever we have tried moves 1-4%; this term is a factor of two.
HONEST CONSEQUENCE: a latent-space fix for the arm tail is unlikely to exist,
because the quantity being fitted is dominated by a transformation that happens
before our model sees the data. The defensible latent-side actions are
(a) stop reporting arm accuracy against a single retarget as if it were truth,
(b) report direction and roll separately so the two failure modes stay visible,
(c) if arm fidelity to the HUMAN is the product requirement, supervise against
the human's arm directions through the robot's own geometry rather than against
the retargeted label.
(c) is a real latent-space change and it is the only one of the three that
could move the number. It is also a different objective from what the model has
been trained on, so it needs its own arm and its own acceptance.
