RESPONSE SPECTRUM (0811) — linear-response normal modes of the latent, and a
shared-basis test.  Frozen Equator A3+B3 + Greenwich C1, zero training.

Analogy stated honestly: no Hamiltonian, no unitary evolution, so "eigenstate"
in the QM sense does not apply. What transfers is LINEAR RESPONSE. J is the
central-difference Jacobian of (rendered motion) w.r.t. the 192 lattice coords,
one lattice cell per step; SVD(J) gives the mode spectrum.

E1 NORMAL MODES / GAUGE
bridge          effective modes (participation ratio, of 192)   near-null dirs
walk->kick                 5.2                                      114

dance->walk                3.4                                      134
punch->jump                9.3                                       93
jacks->run                 7.7                                       94
sit->dance                 7.6                                       94
  => THE HEADLINE: a 192-coordinate latent exposes only ~3-9 EFFECTIVE degrees
  of freedom at any operating point, and roughly HALF the coordinates (93-134)
  are near-null: the code changes, the body does not. That near-null half is a
  GAUGE sector — redundancy the tokenizer carries for free. It explains, with a
  mechanism, two earlier observations: (i) why a random 192-d push sometimes
  scored rho=0.67 in the transfer control (random pushes have large components
  in the few active modes), and (ii) why sampling diversity is modest even at
  high temperature — most token variation is gauge and never reaches the body.

E2 SHARED BASIS (the question the failed transfer test could not answer)
  mean principal angle between top-8 response subspaces, cross-bridge: 69.0 deg
  random-subspace control (200 draws):                       80.0 +- 1.0 deg
  => 11 sigma below chance, but 69 deg is still far from aligned. Reading: the
  bridges share a WEAK common subspace, not a common basis. Single-vector
  transfer failed (previous verdict) and this explains why without rescuing it:
  there is real shared structure, it is just small compared with the
  bridge-specific part. Pairs are heterogeneous (60 deg walk->kick vs
  jacks->run; 75 deg run->sit vs punch->jump), so any shared sector is partial.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE "EMERGENCE INTERPRETABILITY" QUESTION
  There IS a compact, measurable structure: ~5 active modes + ~100 gauge
  directions per operating point. That is a mechanism-level statement about the
  representation, obtained without labels or training. What is NOT established:
  that the active modes mean the same thing across contexts. Until the shared
  subspace is much tighter than 69 deg, "we can read the emergence mechanism"
  is not supported by this evidence.
