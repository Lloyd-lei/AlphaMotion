DISTURBANCE TEST v2 (0812, lloyd's design) [v1 RETRACTED, see below] — a foreign pose pinned mid-flight.
Walking A -> B over n=90, at frame 45 the pose is hard-pinned to a pose from
another family (dance / jump / kick). Goal unchanged. 10 reps, frozen A3+B3+C1.
Endpoint pass-through DISABLED for this test: with the exit pinned, "did it
arrive" is 0.00 by construction and measures nothing.

scenario                     arrive (cm)   jerk
S1 clean A->B                     2.31     0.32
S2 shoved, same goal              2.31     1.27
S3 re-plan, n given (45)          2.41     0.89
S3f re-plan, n INFERRED           2.31     0.73
S4 goal swapped mid-flight        2.71     —
peak detour caused by the shove: 21.2 cm     end deviation vs clean: 0.00 cm

WHAT HOLDS
 * DISTURBANCE REJECTION IS REAL AND FREE. A 21 cm mid-course shove costs
   NOTHING in arrival: 2.31 cm shoved vs 2.31 cm clean, and the end deviation
   from the undisturbed trajectory is 0.00. The bridge absorbs the shove and
   still lands on the goal. This is the closed-loop behaviour the freedom-ratio
   work could only hint at, now demonstrated directly.
 * SWAPPING THE GOAL mid-flight costs 0.4 cm (2.71 vs 2.31). Re-targeting under
   way is nearly free — the moving-target / adversarial setting is architecturally
   supported today, without retraining.
 * n-FREE WORKS, AND IS BETTER. Letting the prior pick its own budget by chain
   NLL gives arrival 2.31 (equal to clean, better than the 2.41 of the supplied
   budget) and the SMOOTHEST recovery of all disturbed arms (jerk 0.73 vs 0.89).
   The chosen budgets spread over the whole grid (30..120, mode at 90) rather
   than collapsing to one value — the model does have an opinion about pacing.
   lloyd's "the target is the identity, n is only pacing" is supported.
 * COST OF THE SHOVE IS SMOOTHNESS, NOT ACCURACY: jerk 0.32 -> 1.27 (4x the
   clean value, ~6x the corpus median 0.205). The recovery is correct but ugly;
   re-planning halves that (0.89), inferring n halves it again (0.73).

READING: the model is a strong disturbance-rejecting planner as long as it has
a goal — which is the exact complement of the autonomous-flow collapse. Give it
a target and it is robust to a 21 cm shove; take the target away and it drifts.
Together these two results say the missing ingredient is INTENTION, not
dynamics, which is lloyd's "发呆" reframe and it changes what E17's RL is for:
supplying intention, not repairing the flow.

=== v1 RETRACTED, v2 RESULTS (0812) ===
RETRACTION. In v1 the disturbance was spliced into the DECODED OUTPUT: one
frame of the produced trajectory was overwritten with a foreign pose. Two
consequences, both fatal to the v1 reading:
  a) the trajectory jumps at that frame by construction (lloyd spotted this in
     the video: curr -> curr+1 must stay continuous even under disturbance);
  b) the model never saw the disturbance at all, so "21 cm shove costs nothing,
     end deviation 0.00" measured nothing about robustness — the rest of the
     trajectory was simply unaffected because nothing had changed for it.
The v1 disturbance-rejection claim is withdrawn.

v2 injects the disturbance where it belongs: as a PINNED TOKEN in the
conditioning (the sparse-pin mode B was trained on), so the model plans around
it. Token j covers timeline segment j; the pinned value is taken from the
foreign clip's own tokenisation.

scenario                     arrive (cm)   jerk    max frame step (cm)
S1 clean A->B                     2.31     0.32          2.91
S2 shoved (token-pinned)          2.30     0.40          3.24
S3 re-plan, n given               2.41     0.89           —
S3f re-plan, n INFERRED           2.31     0.73           —
S4 goal swapped mid-flight        2.71      —             —
peak detour 11.6 cm   end deviation vs clean 0.61 cm

WHAT v2 ACTUALLY SHOWS (and it is a better result than the retracted one)
 * The model DOES plan around the constraint: the trajectory now detours 11.6 cm
   to honour the pinned pose and comes back to within 0.61 cm of the clean
   ending, while arrival is unchanged (2.30 vs 2.31).
 * And it stays SMOOTH while doing it: jerk 0.40 vs 0.32 clean (v1's post-hoc
   splice gave 1.27), and the largest single-frame step is 3.24 cm against the
   clean trajectory's own 2.91 cm — i.e. the disturbed motion's worst step is
   within 11% of what an undisturbed motion already does. lloyd's continuity
   requirement is met once the pin is in the conditioning.
 * n-FREE still wins on the re-plan arms (2.31 vs 2.41 arrival, jerk 0.73 vs
   0.89) and its chosen budgets still spread across 30..120.
So: real disturbance rejection, planned rather than accidental, and smooth.
