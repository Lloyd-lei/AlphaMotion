Peter Thiel once said that a brilliant startup idea must be a secret that no one else knows. I am incredibly grateful to the robotics and AI scientists and engineers present today for sharing so many secrets with me (laughs). That is exactly why I came to IROS—to learn more secrets from the best minds in the world.

Embodied AI—we are witnessing the peak of engineering rise in human history. It is a highly complex science that fuses mechanical engineering, deep learning, electromagnetism, materials science, and computer engineering. Back in 2022, during Unitree’s Series B round, this company—which was benchmarking against Boston Dynamics’ Spot quadruped—caught my attention. That was right around the time I had finished my undergraduate coursework in analytical mechanics and was just getting into reinforcement learning. Back then, I realized that machine learning fully driven by neural networks would very soon replace traditional MPC-based robotics paradigms. I shared this insight with a few investors I knew well. Today, that early insights has already yielded handsome capital returns for my investor friends. I am deeply honored to now be personally participating on the eve of the next explosive cycle of Embodied AI.

I previously served as a founding member of StarBot and participated in the development of their early autonomous robotics solutions. We competed in the first Beijing World Humanoid Robotics Games last year, taking 4th place in the hotel reception scenario and 6th in medicine sorting. We were one of only two teams on-site that delivered a fully autonomous solution, and yes, we beat the Tsinghua team who also brought an autonomous setup. Back then, we were still using traditional computer vision detectors for the perception layer, while the execution layer was entirely handed off to a rule-based control stack. Yet, to my amazement, in just one short year, we have lived through the paradigm shifts from VLA, WAM, and Robotics Agent Harness. I am genuinely thrilled by the booming development of this field, which, of course, wouldn't be possible without the unparalleled research work of the scientists in this room.

Last summer, I participated in early work on the Atlas controller at RAI, a sister institute of Boston Dynamics. Atlas’ Zest showed me the possibility that the control layer of a humanoid robot could be handed over entirely to neural networks to run fully autonomously, and Sonic made me witness a miracle once again. This time around, however, despite an explosion of work deployed on robotic arms, the development speed of fully autonomous intelligence for humanoid robots has been far slower than I anticipated. Quite frankly, it wasn't until the night before I left for IROS that I saw the Agibot team replicate Sonic on their A3. I must say that outside of Figure, NVIDIA GEAR, and Boston Dynamics, I have yet to see any humanoid robotics team catch up to the current level of Sonic in terms of WBC. (As for Tesla’s Optimus Autopilot team of 12 people—I simply don't believe they can deliver on the big picture that Elon Musk is drawing). Of course, I'm not saying there is no WBC work outside of these platforms. For instance, Professor Guanya Shi and his LeCar Lab's recent work, BFM-Zero, shows us a brand-new direction for reinforcement learning. However, the paradigms of most commercial solutions out there are still stuck in the tracking-based RL of expert policies.

I realize that if there is no breakthrough in the low-level controllers of humanoid robots, the public's impression of them will remain that of expensive, backflipping toys on the Spring Festival Gala stage—Unitree's post-IPO stock performance clearly reflects this current public perspective. As of Q3 2026, over $40 billion has been invested in robotics in the United States. Yet, an investor recently told me, "We fools on Sand Hill Road dumped so much money to robotics companies, and yet you still don't see a single working robot on the streets of Palo Alto." I once asked Chenran Li (alias 80g), one of the authors of Sonic, why Sonic wasn't built on platforms other than G1, and why virtually no teams could replicate it on non-G1 hardware after the code was open-sourced last December. I also asked teams working on humanoid loco-manipulation why manipulation data is so abundant, yet we have still never seen a jaw-dropping, major breakthrough in humanoid manipulation capabilities. Their answers all pointed to one critical core: while robots are no longer starved for data—it seems the stories told by many top robotics companies are finally being vindicated—the amount of locomotion and manipulation data that can be effectively utilized on a non-mainstream embodiment remains overwhelmingly out of reach.

Since we are all believers in the scaling law (and I trust no one here isn't), this time, let me add some fuel to the fire. In May of this year, our team accidentally unlocked a brand-new technology: a learning-based retargeting tokenizer capable of zero-shot new-embodiment, new-motion, and both locomotion and manipulation. What does this mean? It means a massive legacy pool of Unitree G1 data can be mapped directly to any new robot. It means a massive legacy pool of egocentric UMI and real-world teleop data can be mapped to any new robot. This could mean that the volume of usable data for a single robot scales up by several orders of magnitude. This might mean that by early next year, we will see every humanoid robot in the world equipped with its own WBC. I realized this is the right time—investors always ask the "why now" question—a crucial window to pivot the public and market perspective of humanoid robots. So, I called up a few of my investor friends, and they chose to believe in me again.

During IROS and CoRL, our team plans to open-source the largest cross-embodiment robot dataset in the history of the industry with full throttle—500,000 hours. 500,000 hours. Huge thanks to my data vendor friends who chose to trust me. This data will be completely free for academic and non-commercial deployment. 500,000 hours, but it doesn't stop there. When I say 500,000 hours of data, I mean raw data. We plan to expand all of this data to cover 20 different embodiments, including Unitree G1, H2, Agibot X2, A3, Fourier GR3, and Booster T3. Believe me, if you hand me a brand-new URDF file today, I can map all 500,000 hours onto it. To achieve this goal, all 128 of our GB200s are currently running at full throttle. I believe in the aesthetic of brute force, just as I believe in the scaling law.

We welcome all research teams worldwide to use this data—and remember, it is completely free. If you feel that your current compute resources are insufficient to scale, we are more than willing to provide our own GB200s and H200s for you to use free of charge. I am incredibly eager to contribute what little I can to academia and the industry. I believe that through our collective efforts, next year in Palo Alto—no, scratch that—in every factory and on every single street, we will very soon see fully autonomous humanoid robots everywhere.

So everyone, the wheels to spin up the new era of Embodied AI are in your hands, and I want to turn the wheels with you guys together.
